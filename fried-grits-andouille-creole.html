Fried grits with andouille Creole sauce

Wednesday, Louisiana week, stove only, 30 minutes from 6:00. Sunday's set cheese grits cut into squares and fried crisp in the cast iron, topped with andouille in a quick Creole tomato sauce made from Wednesday's bag of trinity.

Notes

Equipment: 12 inch cast iron skillet · small saucepan · thin metal spatula · infrared thermometer · probe thermometer.

Crisp grits: cold, firm grits fry best. Pat them dry and dust with flour; then leave them alone for the full first side. A crust that sticks is not ready to turn. If the dish is wider than the pan, fry in two batches and set Eating at 10 minutes later.

Frying heat: canola at 375 to 400°F on the infrared, read on the oil. Canola smokes from about 400°F, so lower the burner if it climbs.

Food safety: andouille comes cooked; browning heats it through. Bring the sauce to 165°F on a probe before serving, as with any leftover or carried dish.

Tonight, after dinner: move Thursday's ground meat from the freezer to the fridge on a plate. It thaws flat overnight and is ready by 6:00 tomorrow.

Mild for the boys: the Cajun seasoning amount keeps it gentle and nothing else hot goes in the pot. Hot sauce stays on the table for the Captain.

No Cajun seasoning? For each 1 tsp: 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, 1/4 tsp chili powder, 1/8 tsp dried oregano, 1/8 tsp ground cumin and a pinch of kosher salt.

No thyme? Use the same amount of dried oregano.

No hot sauce? A pinch of chili powder over his bowl, or a pinch of chili powder stirred into a spoon of cider vinegar.

Salt: the kosher salt amounts suit a light, flaky kosher salt. A dense, fine kosher salt is about twice as salty by volume: use half.

Infrared readings: infrared reads the surface only. Cast iron reads true; a shiny saucepan reads low, so aim at the oil or the food. Aim close, straight down, and read two or three spots, as pans vary from edge to centre. Liquids (stock, gravy, sauce, grits) go by a probe.
