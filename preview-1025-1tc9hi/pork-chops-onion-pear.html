Cast iron pork chops, onion and pear pan sauce

Bone-in chops seared hard in cast iron after the onions have gone slowly golden in the same pan with butter. Pears in with the onions, the fond lifted with apple cider, a knob of butter to finish, over the house mash on the burner next door. Fresh off the heat at 6:30. Set the time you want to eat and everything runs itself backward.

Notes

The mash is the site's mashed potatoes, the same amounts and steps at 3 lb, on its own two burners beside the skillet.

Food safety: pork chops to 145°F on a probe in the centre, not touching bone, then at least a 3 minute rest. They rest 5 to 6 minutes while the sauce finishes and go out fresh off the heat.

Apple cider here is the drink, not the vinegar. The teaspoon of cider vinegar at the end sharpens the sauce.

Stock: the chicken stock is made up from chicken stock powder, 1 teaspoon to each cup of hot water.

Salt: the kosher salt amounts are sized for Diamond Crystal. Morton is about twice as salty by volume: use half.

Infrared readings: the gun reads only the surface it points at, never the inside of the food or the depth of a liquid. Cast iron and dark, seasoned pans read true; shiny stainless and aluminum read low, so aim at the oil, the fat or a dark spot. Aim close, since the spot it reads grows with distance, and read two or three spots: pans vary a lot from edge to centre. On a grill, aim at a grate bar, not the coals. Simmering water, brine and deep oil get a probe thermometer instead. Oven and smoker numbers are the dial settings.
