Chicken broth

Monday, Louisiana week, on the stove while he works from home (the Instant Pot has the gumbo all day). Two frozen rotisserie chicken carcasses go in frozen with cold water, the spare onion, two spare celery ribs and a bay leaf. Up to a bare simmer, skimmed, then held 4 hours 40 minutes at 190 to 200°F. Strained about 1:30, cooled fast in an ice bath, in jars and in the fridge by 2:40. No salt: season it in the recipes. It goes into Wednesday's Creole sauce and Thursday's dirty rice.

Notes

Equipment: stockpot, 8 quart if you have one (the big 5 to 6 quart pot holds two carcasses with a little less water) · fine mesh strainer · large metal bowl or a second pot that fits in the sink · ice from the freezer · ladle and tongs · jars with lids · probe thermometer.

Yield: about 8 to 10 cups from two carcasses, about 4 to 5 from one. An estimate: it depends on the pot and how hard it simmers.

Bare simmer: 190 to 200°F on a probe, a bubble or two breaking now and then, never a boil. A boil clouds the broth and drives off water. The lid sits ajar.

Do not use the trinity bags: they are Wednesday's and Thursday's. The broth gets the fifth onion and two loose celery ribs from the bunch.

Cooling, the food safety rule: under 70°F within 2 hours of the heat going off, then under 40°F within the next 4 hours. The ice bath does the first part in about an hour; the fridge does the rest, so leave space around the jars.

Keeps: about 4 days in the fridge, so Wednesday and Thursday are fine, or about 3 months in the freezer (leave 1 inch at the top of the jar). The fat cap on top is optional: leave it as a seal, or lift it off cold.

No broth? Wednesday's and Thursday's pages carry water plus powdered chicken stock as the fallback, cup for cup.
