Rotisserie chicken broth

Instant Pot, pressure cooked. Frozen rotisserie chicken carcasses go straight in with the spare onion, celery, a bay leaf and cold water to the PC MAX line, then Pressure Cook High 90 minutes and a natural release. Strained into an ice bath, into jars and the fridge. Set the time you start and every step counts forward from it: a 2:47 start strains about 5:10 and is in the fridge by 6:20. The come-up and release times are estimates.

Notes

Equipment: 6 quart Instant Pot with its sealing lid · tongs · oven gloves · fine mesh sieve · large metal bowl that fits in the sink · ice from the freezer · ladle · jars with lids · probe thermometer.

The clock is an estimate: from frozen the pot takes about 25 minutes to come up to pressure (it can be 25 to 35), and the natural release about 20 minutes (it can be 20 to 30). The page uses 25 and 20 so a 2:47 start strains about 5:10. If the pin is slow either time, the steps after it run late by the same; press Start now on the next step to re-time the rest.

PC MAX line: fill only to the PC MAX line on the inner pot, two thirds full, never the top line. Three carcasses only if they fit under it broken up; the water then stays at the line, so three gives about the same volume as two, just richer.

Yield: about 8 to 10 cups from two carcasses, about 5 to 6 from one, about 9 to 10 from three. An estimate. No salt in it: the recipes that use it season it.

Cooling, the food safety rule: under 70°F within 2 hours of straining, then under 40°F within the next 4 hours. The ice bath does the first part in about an hour; the fridge does the rest, so leave space around the jars.

Keeps: about 4 days in the fridge, or about 3 months in the freezer with 1 inch of space at the top of the jar. The fat cap on top is optional: leave it as a seal, or lift it off cold.

No broth? The pages that use it carry water plus powdered chicken stock as the fallback, cup for cup.
