Carne guisada

Monday night, Instant Pot. One chuck pot roast in 1.5 inch cubes, seared hard in the cast iron, onion and bell pepper softened in the drippings with garlic, chili powder, cumin and oregano, then 25 minutes under pressure in tomato and beef stock. The gravy is thickened with a flour slurry at the end, so the cubes hold their shape and keep some bite. Rice cooker at 5:05, tortillas off the cast iron at the end. Mild in the pot, jalapeno on the side. Set the time you want to eat and every step runs itself backward.

Notes

Why the cast iron, not Saute: the cast iron holds 400 to 425°F through a batch of cold beef and reads true on the infrared; the Instant Pot's thin steel drops when the meat goes in, reads low, and steams more than it sears. The drippings and the deglaze carry every brown bit into the pot.

Bite, not mush: 1.5 inch cubes, 25 minutes on High and a 15 minute natural release leaves them tender but whole. The flour goes in at the end as a slurry, not before, so nothing scorches on the bottom under pressure and the gravy thickens in 5 minutes without stewing the beef further. Stir the slurry in gently.

Salt, counted: the last pressure cook was a touch salty, so this one carries 3/4 tsp of kosher salt on the beef at 4 servings, 1 tsp of beef bouillon paste in 1 cup of water (the usual 1 to 1, just less of it), and the salt already in the tomato sauce. That is about 2,900 mg of sodium for a 2.27 lb roast, on the gentle side. The last 1/4 tsp is only for a gravy that tastes flat after thickening.

Pepper: never before a sear. Each cube is peppered only on a face that has already seared and is facing up, after each turn, and again as it comes off.

Mild for the boys: ordinary mild chili powder, no cayenne. The jalapeno is for his bowl.

Burn warning: stock first, beef on it, tomato sauce poured over the top and not stirred in: thick tomato on the bottom is what throws a Burn warning. If it shows, Cancel, valve to Venting, scrape the bottom, add 1/4 cup water and start the 25 minutes again.

Size: the clock is set for 4 servings, about 2 1/4 lb, seared in two batches. At 6 to 8 sear in three batches and set Eating at 10 minutes later; the 25 minutes stay the same, as cube size sets the time. The servings stop at 3 to keep enough liquid in the Instant Pot.

Done temperature: pressure cooking takes the beef far past the 145°F safe point; the spoon test is the cue for tenderness. Leftovers into the fridge within 2 hours.

Infrared readings: infrared reads the surface only. Cast iron reads true. Aim close, straight down, and read two or three spots, as pans vary from edge to centre. Liquids, like the gravy, go by a probe.
