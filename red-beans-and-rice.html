Cajun red beans and rice

Under 30 minutes on Tuesday night: the beans get a quick soak in the Instant Pot while the sausage and the trinity brown in cast iron, then it all goes into the Instant Pot insert and the fridge. Wednesday morning, stock and Cajun seasoning in, a 10 minute hard boil for safety, then Slow Cook before you leave. Home at 5:00, rice in the rice cooker, dinner at 6:30. Mild for the boys, hot sauce on the side. Set the time you want to eat and every step, Tuesday night's included, runs itself backward.

Notes

Why the Wednesday boil: raw kidney beans carry a toxin (phytohaemagglutinin, a lectin) that only a hard boil destroys. A slow cooker on low, and the Instant Pot's Slow Cook most of all, may never reach a boil, and beans heated but not boiled can be more toxic than raw. Tuesday's 2 minute pressure cook is a quick soak and may not be enough on its own, so the FDA's rule is kept: at least 10 minutes at a full rolling boil, timed from the boil, before Slow Cook. It also takes the cold pot from the fridge straight up to heat instead of letting it creep up all morning. Canned kidney beans are already cooked and safe.

Two nights, three tools: Tuesday the Instant Pot quick soaks the beans while the cast iron browns the sausage and trinity, and it all ends up in the Instant Pot insert in the fridge. Wednesday the Instant Pot does the boil and the slow cook, and the rice cooker does the rice.

Size: a 6 quart Instant Pot holds up to 4 servings (1 lb of beans). For 6 or 8, use an 8 quart pot, or the soak runs past the half line and the boil foams over.

Beans still firm at the finish? Old beans and hard water slow them down. Keep simmering on Saute Less, lid off, stirring, until they crush easily; push Eating at back to match.

Heat: 1/8 teaspoon of cayenne for the whole pot, plus whatever the sausage brings. Smoked sausage is the mild choice; andouille is the option for a spicier pot.

Infrared readings: the gun reads only the surface it points at, never the inside of the food or the depth of a liquid. Cast iron and dark, seasoned pans read true; shiny stainless and aluminum read low, so aim at the oil, the fat or a dark spot. Aim close, since the spot it reads grows with distance, and read two or three spots: pans vary a lot from edge to centre. On a grill, aim at a grate bar, not the coals. Simmering water, brine and deep oil get a probe thermometer instead. Oven and smoker numbers are the dial settings.
