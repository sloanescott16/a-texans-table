Grillades and grits

Sunday, Louisiana week. A beef chuck tender roast sliced across the grain into medallions and pounded thin, seared hard in the cast iron, then braised 2 hours in the Dutch oven in a dark roux gravy with the trinity, tomatoes and beef stock, over cheese grits made with stock and butter, green beans on the side. While it braises, the week's prep: a double dark roux (half for tonight, half for Monday), the trinity chopped and bagged for all four nights, a second pot of grits set in a dish for Wednesday, and the week's chicken broth made in the Instant Pot from two frozen carcasses. First move at 2:20 for 6:30.

Notes

Equipment: 12 inch cast iron skillet for the sear · Dutch oven with its lid, for the roux and the braise · big pot, 5 to 6 quart, for the grits · small saucepan for the beans · Instant Pot with its sealing lid, for the broth · sieve, bowl and ice for cooling the broth · jars with lids · heatproof steel or glass bowl for the hot roux · 8 by 8 or 9 by 9 inch square pan, so Wednesday's squares cut evenly · three zip bags · meat mallet or rolling pin · infrared thermometer · probe thermometer.

The week from tonight: Monday's half of the roux is in a covered bowl in the fridge. The broth is in jars in the fridge: Monday's gumbo takes 5 cups, Wednesday 1/2 cup and Thursday 1/2 cup, 6 cups in all against an estimated 8 to 10. Wednesday's grits are set in the dish. The trinity bags are Monday, Wednesday and Thursday.

Ground meat for Thursday: raw ground meat keeps only 1 to 2 days in the fridge, so it goes in the freezer tonight, pressed flat in its bag. Wednesday's page moves it to the fridge after dinner.

Roux safety: a dark roux is near 350°F and sticks to skin. Long sleeves, no water near the pan, and stir with a flat wooden spatula. Black specks or a sharp, acrid burnt smell mean start again; a scorched roux cannot be saved.

Roux heat: hold the roux at 325 to 350°F on the infrared, read on the roux itself. Canola smokes from about 400°F, so never let it pass 375°F. About 40 minutes takes it to dark chocolate. If it darkens faster than the slots, lower the burner and keep stirring: colour is the cue, the clock is the guide. The roux is always made in the Dutch oven, never the cast iron skillet; the cast iron sears, then is deglazed into the stock.

Broth: Pressure Cook High 90 minutes and a natural release give about 8 to 10 cups from two carcasses (an estimate). No salt in it: the recipes season it. Cool it fast: under 70°F within 2 hours of straining and under 40°F within the next 4. It keeps about 4 days in the fridge, so Thursday is fine, or 3 months frozen with 1 inch of space at the top of the jar. Fill only to the PC MAX line, two thirds, never the top line.

Food safety: chuck tender braised 2 hours is far past the 145°F safe point; the fork test and 195 to 205°F inside are the cues for tender. Wednesday's grits go into the fridge in a shallow dish within 30 minutes and keep 3 to 4 days. Leftovers into the fridge within 2 hours, moved out of the Dutch oven into a container first.

Grits: water with powdered chicken stock, then butter and sharp cheddar at the end. Use regular or quick grits, not instant; if the bag says longer than 25 minutes, go by the bag and set Eating at later.

Size: set for 3 servings, one beef chuck tender roast of about 2.75 lb (this one is 2.77 lb; use or freeze by 10 Nov) in three sear batches. At 5 or 6, sear in four batches and set Eating at 6 minutes later; the Dutch oven needs to be 5 quart or larger for the braise. The servings scale the week's trinity too; the broth is two carcasses either way.

Mild for the boys: the Cajun seasoning amount keeps it gentle and nothing else hot goes in the pot. Hot sauce stays on the table for the Captain.

Pepper: never on raw meat before a sear. Pepper goes on each seared face after it turns up, and on the last side as it comes off.

No Cajun seasoning? For each 1 tsp: 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, 1/4 tsp chili powder, 1/8 tsp dried oregano, 1/8 tsp ground cumin and a pinch of kosher salt.

No thyme? Use the same amount of dried oregano.

No hot sauce? A pinch of chili powder over his bowl, or a pinch of chili powder stirred into a spoon of cider vinegar.

Salt: the kosher salt amounts suit a light, flaky kosher salt. A dense, fine kosher salt is about twice as salty by volume: use half.

Infrared readings: infrared reads the surface only. Cast iron reads true; a shiny saucepan reads low, so aim at the oil or the food. Aim close, straight down, and read two or three spots, as pans vary from edge to centre. Liquids (stock, gravy, sauce, grits) go by a probe.
