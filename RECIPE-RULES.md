# Recipe authoring rules (A Texan's Table)

Standing rules from the Captain. Every new or edited recipe page follows all of them.

1. One cooking device per method. A step names the one pan, pot, grill or appliance it uses.
2. Every step is timed.
3. Step cards: the step's ingredients on top, then the instruction.
4. Full seasoning measurements stay on the page. Never "season to taste" alone where an amount is known.
5. No em dashes.
6. Heat cues carry a number (the Captain, 16 Oct 2026: "give me the specific temperature we're looking for. I have a little infrared radar gun ... Let's get scientific").
   - Every vague heat cue (get the pan hot, until the oil shimmers, medium-high, hot grill, preheat the skillet, smoking hot) gets a target surface temperature in °F for an infrared thermometer. Keep the old cue alongside where it helps, for example "until the pan reads 400 to 425°F on the infrared, oil shimmering".
   - Infrared reads the surface only. Shiny stainless and aluminum read low: take the reading on the oil, the fat or a dark spot. Cast iron reads true. On a grill, aim at a grate bar, not the coals.
   - Liquids (simmering water, sauces, brine) and deep oil get a probe temperature, said as "on a probe".
   - Oven and smoker temperatures stay as the dial setting.
   - Every page with an infrared cue carries the short "Infrared readings" note at the end of its notes, including: aim close, and read two or three spots, pans vary edge to centre.
   - Name an oil that suits the heat. Extra virgin olive oil smokes from about 375°F; butter burns past about 300°F, so it goes in once the pan has dropped below that.
   - Raw egg sauces (aioli, bacon fat mayo): pasteurized eggs, refrigerate at once, use within 2 days.
   - Pork sausage cooks to 160°F inside, pork chops to 145°F, on a probe.
   - Poultry, including chicken thighs, to 165°F minimum on a probe in the thickest part, not touching bone; thighs are often taken to 175 to 185°F for texture. Ground meat: 160°F for beef or pork, 165°F if any poultry. Never "juices run clear": juice colour is not a safe test (USDA, 17 Oct 2026).
7. Per-side timers (the Captain, 16 Oct 2026). Any sear or flip step gives each side its own timer he can start, finish and move on to the next side. Recipe timers cover every step he is told to do. (Built first on pot roast; the other recipes follow in a later job.)

## Working numbers (surface, °F)

These are the ranges used so far, checked by the researcher on 16 Oct 2026 (research\heat-temps-2026-10-16.md in the kitchen build folder; smoke points from the Wikipedia smoke point table, infrared caveats from ThermoWorks). Use them for new recipes unless the dish needs something else.
- Hard sear, cast iron: 400 to 425 with refined or light olive oil or a neutral oil (not extra virgin). 450 to 500 needs avocado oil or a dry pan with no oil.
- Saute, stainless or cast iron, oil for shrimp or mushrooms: 350 to 375; butter in only once the pan is under 300.
- Medium (aromatics, onions, after a sear): 300 to 350.
- Medium-low (garlic with no color, slow render): 250 to 300.
- Bacon and lardons from a cold pan, medium: climbing to 325 to 350.
- Butter for a roux, foaming not browning: 250 to 300.
- Shallow popping fat (coconut oil, tallow): 375 to 400, never above 400; the test kernels are the cue.
- Deep fry: 350 to 375 on a probe rated for 400 or more, never the infrared.
- Browning sausage or other fatty meat: 325 to 375.
- Grill, direct sear on lump: grate 450 to 550; a violent sear pile, 550 to 650, turn as soon as the char forms and judge doneness by probe; cool side 250 to 350; medium grill 350 to 400. Bacon wrapped or fatty food: indirect or medium-low against flare-ups.
- Warm fat for an egg emulsion (aioli, bacon mayo): 100 to 120.
- Liquids on a probe: gentle simmer 180 to 190, lively or brisk simmer 195 to 205, boil 212 at sea level; pickling brine dissolved at 160 to 180.
- Egg sauce (carbonara): pan under 150 and off the heat before the eggs; sauce done at 160, never above 165, stirring off the heat.

## Plain ingredient names (the Captain, 19 Oct 2026)

"It's a low sodium chicken stock because I'll just spend all your time looking for it. Just say chicken stock. And use that for other things, right? Like recipes. Recipes need to stop being so specific. Just grab what you can get and get on with it."

- Ingredients use plain generic names: chicken stock, smoked sausage, yellow onions, kosher salt, pork shoulder, apples, ground beef.
- No brand names and no buying qualifiers like "low-sodium", "Diamond Crystal", "80/20", "Boston butt", "Honeycrisp or Gala", "Valentina or Cholula". Keep a qualifier only when it really changes the dish: bone-in or boneless, skin-on, dry or canned beans, 6 inch or 8 inch tortillas, mild vs hot sausage, unsalted butter, block cheese that has to melt. A brand stays only where the brand is the dish (Cafe du Monde cold brew, Flavacol theater popcorn, Velveeta in the mac and cheese, Coca-Cola in the chashu braise).
- Full measurements stay. Where an amount depends on a brand (Diamond Crystal kosher salt is about half as salty by volume as Morton), keep the amount, call it "kosher salt", and leave the "Morton: use half" note in the page notes.
- Shopping lists: the item and roughly how much to buy, no "need X tsp" notes.
- Meat on shopping lists (8 Nov 2026): only what every ordinary grocery store stocks, written loosely with swaps built in (chicken thighs, boneless if they have them but any are fine; ground pork, ground beef or breakfast sausage), never a specific cut. Cut-dependent handling lives in the recipe as a short branch per option.

## Roux (the Captain, 8 Nov 2026)

- Roux is never made in the cast iron skillet, always in the Dutch oven. The cast iron sears, then is deglazed with some of the stock, which goes back into the stock jug with the fond.

## Pages and the cook's evening (the Captain, 8 Nov 2026)

"This page is like 6 recipes on one. And does it have me cooking for 4 hours straight." "Step one is its own thing. Not part of this recipe."

- One dinner per page. Nothing for other nights goes on a dinner's page.
- A side cook like stock or broth always gets its own recipe page, never folded into a dinner's page.
- Prep for another night is its own short optional page or nothing. The plan never front-loads a night. Overlap between nights means one shop and zero-cost components (half a roux into a bowl, extra rice in the same cooker), never extra chopping or bagging for later nights. Each night dices its own trinity.
- On his cook nights he is a solo parent with two young boys. Active time is capped at about an hour on Sunday and 20 to 30 minutes on a weeknight, the rest hands off. Never a 4 hour block.
- Every dinner page states its active time at the top, honestly.

## One line per ingredient (the Captain, 8 Nov 2026)

- An ingredient appears once in a step's list: one line with the total for that step, never one line per night's share or use.
- The same in the page's main ingredient list: one line per ingredient with the total, and the split in words (for example "Sunday 1, Monday 1 1/2, Wednesday 1/2, Thursday 1").
- The shared page code merges lines with the same ingredient name into one scaled total, so this holds on every page.

## Cooking fat (the Captain, 8 Nov 2026)

- He keeps a large supply of beef tallow. Where a recipe calls for canola or another neutral oil for searing or browning, beef tallow is the default and canola is the alternative. Ingredient lists read "beef tallow (or canola oil)" with the same amount, one line per ingredient.
- A roux can be made with tallow too: fine at 325 to 350°F. A tallow roux sets hard when chilled and melts straight back in when warmed.
- Tallow smokes from about 400 to 420°F: sear at 400°F, not above, when using it. Canola can go to 425°F.

## Lessons from Sunday's grillades (the Captain, 8 Nov 2026)

"While tonight tastes good execution wasn't. From the onset you had me destined for failure and I needed to make multiple corrections to get a good meal on the table on time."

- Tight clock, braise in the Instant Pot. When the clock is tight, the pressure route is the default for a braise: the seared meat, its plate juices, the stock and the canned tomatoes go in and are poured on top, not stirred; 20 minutes at high pressure, 10 minutes natural release. Meanwhile the roux base (roux, trinity, garlic, tomato paste, spices) waits in the Dutch oven. Then the hot liquid is whisked into the roux base a cup at a time, the meat goes in, and it gets a 5 to 10 minute lazy simmer. Never pressure cook a roux-thickened liquid: it scorches on the bottom. The long Dutch oven braise stays on the page as the alternative.
- Active time against his real evening. Every dinner page states its active time at the top, and before he gets it the page is sanity checked against his real evening: solo with two young boys, dinner on the table by the stated time. If it does not fit, it is changed before he sees it, not during the cook.
- Exact ingredient names. Say "fresh green beans" or "frozen green beans", never a bare "green beans". The same for anything sold more than one way where the handling differs. Frozen green beans are steamed from frozen, no trimming.
- Roux, real numbers. Working band 325 to 350°F on the infrared, 375°F is the red line, and 360°F is the warning when olive oil is in the mix. A fat-heavy Cajun roux (equal cups of fat and flour, not equal grams) reads lighter than it is: Sunday's took about 70 minutes to reach copper at 325 to 345°F. Pages give colour cues with realistic times at those temperatures, never a dark chocolate roux in 40 minutes. Grillades are fine on a copper roux.
- Grits, real numbers. About 35 minutes all in: about 10 to come to the boil, 25 to cook. His batch is 240 g grits to 6 cups water.

## One plan, and his rice cooker (the Captain, 8 Nov 2026)

"Either you're explaining it poorly or it's a shit recipe. Either way, you need to fix it."

- When he asks how a cook looks, give him one finished plan, never a choice of two. Plans are decided at planning time, not the night before. Pages carry no options or alternatives for the evening.
- His rice cooker takes 45 minutes. On every page the rice goes in 45 minutes before it is needed.

## The weekly menu (the Captain, 11 Nov 2026)

"I think we need to also add a like weekly menu to the kitchen page where I can see all the recipes for the week at once. So I can like proactively make tweaks and I'm not trying to change things on day of."

- week.html ("This week", the first section on the front page) shows every night of the current plan on one page: each cook night's clock and ingredients, read live from its recipe page. Nothing about a recipe is copied into it.
- week.json is the plan: one entry per day, a slug for a cook night, a label for a night with no cooking, "also" for side cooks made that day. Whenever a week is planned or a night changes, update week.json and run `bun tools-gen-week.ts .` so week.html and the front page carry the new week. A week's pages are not finished until the weekly menu shows them.
- Labels for nights with no cooking stay plain ("Eating out", "Off duty"). Nothing about who is home or away goes on the site.
- The weekly menu is where he checks ahead. When he asks for a tweak to a night, change that night's recipe page through its generator, never the weekly menu itself.

## Leftovers he saved (the Captain, 11 Nov 2026)

"I thought it was like fried grit cakes with the leftover grits. I have leftover grits."

- When he saves a leftover for a later night, that night keeps using it. A later rewrite never drops it or swaps the dish without asking him first.
- A question to him that decides what a later page says (is the leftover in the fridge?) stays open until he answers it. No page is rewritten on a guess, and the question goes back to him before the night, not on it.

## Weeknights on his real clock (the Captain, 11 Nov 2026)

"An hour of straight cooking that starts at 5:35 when I'm home alone with two boys really isn't feasible. I'm also not going to pick up the boys and be back home until 5:45, almost 6 most days. So if I start then, we're not eating until 7. Also because you typically have me cooking way faster than I actually cook because I'm not going to be speed running through the kitchen. I think this menu tonight is a failure."

- A weeknight page has no step between the morning and 6:00 pm. He walks in with the boys between 5:45 and 6. The first evening step is at 6:00 or later.
- Step times are written at his pace, not a test kitchen's: every hands-on step gets double the time it would take in a hurry, and the page says "at an easy pace". Waiting on a pan or a pot is not doubled.
- With the steps timed that way, a weeknight dinner lands by 6:30 from a 6:00 start, with about 20 minutes of hands-on work. If it does not fit, the pot does the work during the day (the pattern that works: the insert loaded the night before once the boys are down, Instant Pot on Slow Cook from the morning), or the dish moves to a night with room.
- A page that is over the cap never goes to him with a note saying so. It is changed first, or he is given the choice before it is built.
- Frying in batches is not weeknight work. One pan, with everything else already made.
- A night that falls through is marked as it went on the weekly menu ("Eating out"), and every later night that leaned on it is checked the same evening.

## Quick guide, not a checklist (the Captain, 11 Nov 2026)

"Check list isn't helpful. A quick guide would be but that isn't." "For things like this with a bunch of little things in there, a little checkbox as I go down would be helpful."

- The block at the top of a recipe page is a quick guide, never a checklist of step names. A few blocks, one per stretch of work (the night before, the morning, the evening), each headed by its clock time and how long it takes, each a few plain lines that say what to do with the amounts in them. He can cook from it without scrolling down to the steps.
- It is written per recipe, as the page's `quick` in its generator. Amounts are tokens ({I:id} amount and name, {Q:id} amount alone), so they follow the servings and always match the ingredient list. Block times come from the steps, so they follow Eating at. Every other rule here still holds in it: exact ingredient names, a °F number on every heat cue, no em dashes.
- It fits one phone screen under the sticky bar.
- Every line of a step's "You'll use" list has its own tick box, so a list with a bunch of little things is ticked off line by line as they go in. Ticking a line never ticks the step, and a tap on the instruction still ticks the step.
- The page code for both is in tools-quick-guide-1111.ts (quickGuide and useTicks); a generator calls them as it writes a page, and tools-check-quick-guide-1111.ts is the check. (Built first on the fried grit cakes page; the other recipes follow in a later job.)

## Parts and whole cooks (the Captain, 11 Nov 2026)

"Being able to combine side dishes with a main dish. Because like tomorrow night's dinner, fried grit cakes with andouille creole sauce. Well, that's two recipes ... and technically three, because cheesy grits, fried grit cakes, andouille creole sauce. But you just as easily put this sauce over other stuff. Rice, potatoes, vegetables, peppers ... The site would be able to add all of those recipes together to break out a whole cook."

- A dish that can stand on its own (a main, a side, a sauce, a base like rice or grits) is a part: its own recipe page, written so it goes with anything. Its steps never name another dish. A sauce says "spooned over", not what it is spooned over.
- cook.html ("Build a cook", the second section on the front page) adds parts together into one whole cook: one eating time, one ingredient list with one line per ingredient across all the dishes, and every step from every dish on one clock. It reads each part live from its page, so nothing about a recipe is copied into it. A cook has its own address (cook.html#r=fried-grit-cakes,andouille-creole-sauce) that can be opened, linked or saved to the home screen like any page.
- One pair of hands. In a whole cook no two hands-on steps are ever set for the same minute, and no single pan or pot (the cast iron, the Dutch oven, the Instant Pot, the rice cooker, the big pot) is asked for by two dishes at once. When dishes cannot be made to fit, the page says which dish waits and for how long, or which two clash. It never hides it.
- The dish that waits worst keeps its own timing. A dish that holds (rice on Keep Warm, a sauce in the pot, beans with the lid on) is finished early instead, and never earlier than its hold allows without the page saying so.
- Every step of a part carries "d", its hands-on minutes at his pace (double a hurried pace; waiting on a pan or a pot is not hands-on). A step whose wait may safely run longer (water held at the boil, a pan kept hot) carries "flex", the extra minutes allowed. A free-standing job (a stir, chopping the garnish, tasting) carries "loose", how far it may move either way. The last step of a part is its serve step at 0.
- Every part says how it combines, on its page (the COMBO line) and the same in recipes.json ("combo"): its role, a short tag, its name mid-sentence, how many minutes it holds and how it waits, what it usually goes with, and anything it needs made ahead (fried grit cakes need cheese grits made the day before and set cold).
- A whole cook states its hands-on time at the top, split by when it falls (night before, morning, evening), and it keeps the weeknight rules in view: when the evening's work starts before 6:00 pm it offers "Start at 6:00" and says when dinner lands, and when the evening holds more than 30 minutes of hands-on work it says so before anything is cooked.
- Parts are written by tools-gen-cook-1111.ts (`bun tools-gen-cook-1111.ts .`), which also keeps the list of parts and the ready-made cooks on the front page and in cook.html. A part written by another generator joins in by carrying "combo" on its recipes.json entry and the COMBO line, "d" and the serve step on its page.
- A dinner that is really two or three recipes can be written as its parts, with the dinner as a ready-made cook. Older dinner pages stay as they are until they are split, and the weekly menu still points at one page a night until week.json learns to take a cook.
