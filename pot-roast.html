Pot roast

Classic pot roast, scaled by the weight of meat you have. A hard sear, the aromatics and a red wine deglaze in the cast iron, then the Instant Pot for the rest: pressure in homemade chicken stock and wine, the gravy thickened, the carrots and rutabaga 3 minutes under pressure in it, the meat back in big chunks and everything on Slow Cook until dinner. A garlic and rosemary Yukon Gold mash on the same clock, ready with it.

Notes

The weight of meat drives everything. Put in the weight of chuck you have: every amount, the pressure time, the potato boil and the servings follow it, at about 1/2 lb of raw chuck a serving, so 3 lb serves 6. The page is built on 3 lb in two pieces in a 6 quart Instant Pot, 60 minutes at high. Under 3 lb takes 15 minutes less a pound, over 3 lb 10 more a pound (2 lb 45, 4.5 lb 75), rounded to 5. Over about 4.5 lb use an 8 quart pot and keep the liquid under the max line. One single thick roast instead of two pieces: give it 10 minutes more and tap Start now when the count begins.

The finish, all in the Instant Pot (the Captain, 18 Oct): after the pressure cook and its natural release, meat out, gravy thickened, then the carrots and rutabaga 3 minutes at high pressure in it while the meat is cut into large chunks, the meat back in, and Slow Cook on High until dinner, 1.5 hours by default. Set Slow cook at the top anywhere from 1 to 2 hours and every clock follows it. His cook with the slurry last and only a Slow Cook for the carrots left them undone; the 3 minute burst does the carrots, the Slow Cook brings it all together.

Why rutabaga: onion melts and celery goes soft in a long pressure cook, which is what you want from them in the gravy, but it left the carrots as the only thing with colour. Rutabaga keeps its shape under pressure, its gold flesh sits beside the orange carrot, and its peppery sweetness suits beef; it is the old pot roast root that is not a potato. Cut it smaller than the carrots, since it is denser. Other picks for the same slot, same 3 minutes: parsnips (sweet but pale; 1.5 inch chunks), cremini mushrooms (firm and meaty, but brown; halved), frozen peeled pearl onions (whole), golden beets (earthy and gold; 1 inch). No potatoes: it is served with the mash.

Onions, don’t skimp: they dissolve into the gravy, and that is their job; one was not enough at 3 lb. Celery is up to 5 stalks at 3 lb for the same reason.

Stock: homemade chicken stock is the default, and it is the better pot. Low-sodium beef stock is the alternative. Homemade is usually unsalted, which is what the gravy salt is for; with store-bought stock use half of it and taste.

Two devices, on purpose: the Captain’s method (18 Oct) overrides the one-device rule for this recipe. The sear, the onions and celery, the garlic and paste and the deglaze are in a cast iron skillet on the stove: it browns harder than the Instant Pot’s Saute, and with the fond lifted in the skillet there is far less left to trip the Burn warning. Everything from the pour-in on is in the Instant Pot. The skillet is washed, dried on the burner, oiled and put away while the pressure cycle runs.

Leave by: optional. Set it when you have to head out before dinner. Before you go: the whole roast up to the Slow Cook, and the potatoes peeled into their salted water in the fridge. The Slow Cook runs and drops to Keep Warm on its own. Be home about 45 minutes before Eating at: the mash is made fresh then, so nothing is left in an oven. Keep Warm is capped at 8 hours, since many Instant Pots switch it off at 10 and the roast would cool in the pot; a Leave by earlier than that cooks straight through instead. Blank, the page cooks straight through to dinner.

Doneness: chuck is safe at 145°F with a 3 minute rest (USDA), but it stays tough until the collagen melts at around 200°F. Fork-tender is the test.

Leftovers: fridge within 2 hours, 3 to 4 days covered, ideally sitting in the gravy. Reheat to 165°F.

Infrared readings: the gun reads only the surface it points at, never the inside of the food or the depth of a liquid. Cast iron reads true. The Instant Pot’s inner pot is shiny stainless and reads low on bare metal, so aim at the liquid’s surface only for a rough check and use a probe for liquids. Aim close, since the spot it reads grows with distance, and read two or three spots: pans vary a lot from edge to centre. Simmering water, the butter and cream, and the gravy get a probe thermometer instead. Oven numbers are the dial settings.
