Pot roast

Classic pot roast, scaled by the weight of meat you have. A hard sear and a red wine deglaze in the cast iron, then the Instant Pot for the rest: pressure in homemade chicken stock and wine, then the meat out while the carrots and rutabaga get a 3 minute burst, the meat back in big chunks. A garlic and rosemary Yukon Gold mash on the same clock, ready with it.

Notes

The weight of meat drives everything. Put in the weight of chuck you have: every amount, the pressure time, the potato boil and the servings follow it, at about 1/2 lb of raw chuck a serving, so 3 lb serves 6. The page is built on 3 lb in two pieces in a 6 quart Instant Pot, 60 minutes at high. Under 3 lb takes 15 minutes less a pound, over 3 lb 10 more a pound (2 lb 45, 4.5 lb 75), rounded to 5. Over about 4.5 lb use an 8 quart pot and keep the liquid under the max line. One single thick roast instead of two pieces: give it 10 minutes more and tap Start now when the count begins.

The finish, all in the Instant Pot: meat out, the carrots and rutabaga go into the hot liquid for 3 minutes at high pressure, the meat is cut into large chunks during those minutes, then it goes back in and is stirred through. It replaces the Slow Cook finish, which left the carrots undone after about 2 hours. No device change: the Instant Pot does pressure, release, the veg, the gravy and the Keep Warm hold.

Why rutabaga: onion melts and celery goes soft in a long pressure cook, which is what you want from them in the gravy, but it left the carrots as the only thing with colour. Rutabaga keeps its shape under pressure, its gold flesh sits beside the orange carrot, and its peppery sweetness suits beef; it is the old pot roast root that is not a potato. Cut it smaller than the carrots, since it is denser. Other picks for the same slot, same 3 minutes: parsnips (sweet but pale; 1.5 inch chunks), cremini mushrooms (firm and meaty, but brown; halved), frozen peeled pearl onions (whole), golden beets (earthy and gold; 1 inch). No potatoes: it is served with the mash.

Onions, don’t skimp: they dissolve into the gravy, and that is their job; one was not enough at 3 lb. Celery is up to 5 stalks at 3 lb for the same reason.

Stock: homemade chicken stock is the default, and it is the better pot. Low-sodium beef stock is the alternative. Homemade is usually unsalted, which is what the gravy salt is for; with store-bought stock use half of it and taste.

The cast iron is only for the sear and the deglaze. It browns harder than the Instant Pot’s Saute, and every drop of the pan goes into the pot. It is washed, dried on the burner, oiled and put away while the pressure cycle runs.

Leave by: optional. Set it when you have to head out before dinner. Everything up to the pressure cook and the whole mash is done before you go: the roast cooks, releases and sits on Keep Warm in the Instant Pot on its own, the mash holds covered in a warm oven, and the cut veg wait in the fridge. Be home about 22 minutes before Eating at for the finish. Blank, the page cooks straight through to dinner.

Doneness: chuck is safe at 145°F with a 3 minute rest (USDA), but it stays tough until the collagen melts at around 200°F. Fork-tender is the test.

Leftovers: fridge within 2 hours, 3 to 4 days covered, ideally sitting in the gravy. Reheat to 165°F.

Infrared readings: the gun reads only the surface it points at, never the inside of the food or the depth of a liquid. Cast iron and dark, seasoned pans read true; shiny stainless and aluminum read low, so aim at the oil, the fat or a dark spot. Aim close, since the spot it reads grows with distance, and read two or three spots: pans vary a lot from edge to centre. Simmering water, the butter and cream, and the gravy get a probe thermometer instead. Oven numbers are the dial settings.
