Pot roast

Classic pot roast. A hard sear and a red wine deglaze in the cast iron, then everything into the Instant Pot for 60 minutes under pressure in wine and broth, then the gravy thickened in the pot and the carrots and roast left in it on Slow Cook until dinner, and a Yukon Gold mash on the stove timed to finish with it.

Notes

Times are for about 3 lb of chuck in two pieces in a 6 quart Instant Pot, which is the 6 servings setting. The servings buttons scale the shopping, not the clock. For 2 to 4 servings a smaller roast needs about 45 minutes at high pressure; at 8 servings, about 4 lb, give it 70; for 12 you need an 8 quart pot or two batches, about 80 minutes, and the liquid must stay under the max line. If you set a longer time, tick the steps as you go and tap Start now when the cycle ends: every clock after it catches up. Check by feel.

The cast iron is only for the sear and the deglaze. It browns harder than the Instant Pot’s Saute, and every drop of the pan goes into the pot. The cook itself, pressure, release, gravy, the carrots’ Slow Cook and the Keep Warm hold, is all in the Instant Pot, and the cast iron is washed, dried on the burner, oiled and put away while the pressure cycle runs.

Leave by: the time you have to head out before dinner. Everything hands-on is done by then: the roast and carrots slow cook in the gravy in the Instant Pot, timed to end at Eating at and drop to Keep Warm, the mash is covered in a warm oven, and at Eating at you only slice and serve. Clear it to cook straight through to dinner.

Why the carrots go in last: cooked the full 60 minutes under pressure they turn to mush. In 2 inch chunks, in the hot gravy on Slow Cook More, they go tender with some bite in an hour or more, so the page never sets the Slow Cook shorter than 1 hour; if Leave by is too close to Eating at for that, the plan starts earlier instead. Past 3 hours they only get softer, so the page stops the Slow Cook at 3 hours and Keep Warm holds the rest. If your pot sets Slow Cook only in half hours, round up to the next half hour. It may finish a few minutes after Eating at and simply sits ready, and the extra time only makes the carrots more tender.

Doneness: chuck is safe at 145F with a 3 minute rest (USDA), but it stays tough until the collagen melts at around 200F. Fork-tender is the test.

Leftovers: fridge within 2 hours, 3 to 4 days covered, ideally sitting in the gravy. Reheat to 165F.
