Dirty rice

Thursday, Louisiana week, stove only, 30 minutes from 6:00. Ground pork browned hard in the cast iron with Thursday's bag of trinity, garlic and Cajun spice, then Monday's cold rice fried through it with a splash of stock and soy. Green beans on the side. The pork moves from the freezer to the fridge after Wednesday's dinner.

Notes

Equipment: 12 inch cast iron skillet · small saucepan · flat wooden spatula · infrared thermometer · probe thermometer.

Cold rice is the point: Monday's rice, cooled and dried in the fridge, fries in separate grains. Break up the clumps with your hands before it goes in.

Food safety: ground pork to 160°F on a probe; it is past that after browning and the trinity. Leftover rice must come back to 165°F on a probe, so the rice step ends with a probe. The pork thaws in the fridge, never on the counter.

Still frozen at 6:00? Seal the bag and sit it in a bowl of cold water, changing the water every 30 minutes, and set Eating at later. Never warm water.

Mild for the boys: the Cajun seasoning amount keeps it gentle and nothing else hot goes in the pot. Hot sauce stays on the table for the Captain.

Pepper: never on raw meat before a sear. Pepper goes on each seared face after it turns up, and on the last side as it comes off.

No Cajun seasoning? For each 1 tsp: 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, 1/4 tsp chili powder, 1/8 tsp dried oregano, 1/8 tsp ground cumin and a pinch of kosher salt.

No thyme? Use the same amount of dried oregano.

No hot sauce? A pinch of chili powder over his bowl, or a pinch of chili powder stirred into a spoon of cider vinegar.

Salt: the kosher salt amounts are sized for Diamond Crystal. Morton is about twice as salty by volume: use half.

Infrared readings: infrared reads the surface only. Cast iron reads true; a shiny saucepan reads low, so aim at the oil or the food. Aim close, straight down, and read two or three spots, as pans vary from edge to centre. Liquids (stock, gravy, sauce, grits) go by a probe.
