Chicken and andouille gumbo

Monday, Louisiana week. The Captain's own gumbo, written lean: run it his way. Dutch oven on the stove from 4:45 at a normal afternoon pace. Sunday's dark roux from the fridge, Monday's bag of trinity, Sunday's homemade broth, chicken thighs and half the andouille, simmered about an hour. Rice cooker at 5:15 with Thursday's share.

Notes

His recipe: this is the Captain's gumbo. The steps and temperatures are a guide for the evening; where he does it differently, his way wins.

Equipment: Dutch oven with its lid · rice cooker · sheet pan for cooling Thursday's rice · whisk · tongs · infrared thermometer · probe thermometer.

Bone-in or boneless: either works, skin off. Boneless go in as 1.5 inch pieces. Bone-in go in whole, about 2 1/4 lb for 2 lb boneless, are probed near the bone, and come off the bone at 6:10.

Food safety: chicken to 165°F minimum on a probe in the thickest piece, near the bone but not touching it if bone-in. Andouille comes cooked. Leftovers out of the Dutch oven into a container and into the fridge within 2 hours.

Broth: 5 cups of Sunday's homemade broth; Wednesday and Thursday take 1/2 cup each, inside the 8 to 10 cups it makes. No broth: the same amount of water plus the powdered chicken stock.

Thursday's rice: cooked rice must cool fast. Spread it thin on a sheet pan, then into a covered container in the fridge within the hour. It keeps 3 to 4 days.

Mild for the boys: the Cajun seasoning amount keeps it gentle and nothing else hot goes in the pot. Hot sauce stays on the table for the Captain.

No Cajun seasoning? For each 1 tsp: 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, 1/4 tsp chili powder, 1/8 tsp dried oregano, 1/8 tsp ground cumin and a pinch of kosher salt.

No thyme? Use the same amount of dried oregano.

No hot sauce? A pinch of chili powder over his bowl, or a pinch of chili powder stirred into a spoon of cider vinegar.

Salt: the kosher salt amounts suit a light, flaky kosher salt. A dense, fine kosher salt is about twice as salty by volume: use half.

Infrared readings: infrared reads the surface only. Cast iron reads true; a shiny saucepan reads low, so aim at the oil or the food. Aim close, straight down, and read two or three spots, as pans vary from edge to centre. Liquids (stock, gravy, sauce, grits) go by a probe.
