Chicken and andouille gumbo

Monday, Louisiana week. Loaded on Sunday: Sunday's dark roux whisked into chicken stock with the trinity, andouille, chicken thighs, garlic and Cajun spice, in the Instant Pot inner pot in the fridge. One step at 7:00 am: inner pot in, Slow Cook on High 6 hours, then it holds on Keep Warm. Rice cooker on at 5:00 with a double batch, half kept for Thursday's dirty rice. Back to a simmer, chicken shredded, green onion and parsley on top.

Notes

Equipment: Instant Pot (inner pot loaded on Sunday) with its glass lid or the sealing lid on Venting · rice cooker · sheet pan for cooling Thursday's rice · probe thermometer.

Settings: Slow Cook "More" is the Instant Pot's High. Six hours from a cold, full pot cooks the chicken through and tender; Keep Warm then holds above 145°F for about 4 hours, 1:00 to 5:05, until the Saute step. If the pot came straight from the fridge it is meant to: do not let it sit out first.

Food safety: chicken to 165°F minimum on a probe in the thickest piece; after 6 hours it is far past. Starting on High is the USDA advice for slow cooking. Time to a safe temperature: a fridge-cold full pot on Slow Cook More (High) takes about 3 to 4 hours to pass 140°F, inside the USDA's 4 hour guide. Probe 165°F before serving. The Saute simmer at 5:00 brings the whole pot back to a lively simmer before it is served.

Thursday's rice: cooked rice must cool fast. Spread it thin on a sheet pan, then into a covered container in the fridge within the hour. It keeps 3 to 4 days, so Thursday is fine.

If the gumbo spits on Saute: drop to Saute Less and stir. A thin gumbo is the Cajun style; 20 minutes lid off thickens it a little.

Mild for the boys: the Cajun seasoning amount keeps it gentle and nothing else hot goes in the pot. Hot sauce stays on the table for the Captain.

No hot sauce? A pinch of chili powder over his bowl, or a pinch of chili powder stirred into a spoon of cider vinegar.

Salt: the kosher salt amounts suit a light, flaky kosher salt. A dense, fine kosher salt is about twice as salty by volume: use half.
